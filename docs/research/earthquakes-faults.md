## 1. Catalogues

### 1.1 USGS ComCat via the FDSN event web service

- Endpoint and docs: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/ . The docs state the `limit` parameter accepts 1–20000 and "The service limits queries to 20000"; output formats `csv`, `geojson`, `kml`, `quakeml`/`xml`, `text`; a `count` method takes the same parameters as `query` (text, geojson, xml). No rate limit and no licence statement on the page (the page only links to the generic USGS legal page). (Accessed 2026-10-21.)
- Licence: "USGS-authored or produced data and information are considered to be in the U.S. Public Domain" and "we ask that proper credit be given" (https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/copyrights-and-credits, accessed 2026-10-21).
- **Probe counts for the box 4–22 N, 116–128 E** (https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/count?format=text&minlatitude=4&maxlatitude=22&minlongitude=116&maxlongitude=128&… run 2026-10-21):

| Query | Count |
|---|---|
| all events since 1900-01-01 (no magnitude floor) | 27,334 |
| M ≥ 4 since 1900 | 24,990 |
| M ≥ 4 since 1970 | 24,408 |
| M ≥ 4 since 2000 | 16,213 |
| M ≥ 5 since 1900 | 5,056 |
| M ≥ 6 since 1900 | 631 |
| M ≥ 6.5 since 1900 | 232 |

  So the full PH catalogue is 1.4× the 20,000 per-request cap: two time-split requests (or one per decade) fetch everything. Pre-1970 rows: 736 (probe, CSV 1900–1970), of which 579 are `mw` from the ISC-GEM import (`iscgem…` ids) and 153 have no magnitude type; the box only has 1 event in 1901, 1 in 1903, then 4–31 per year to 1944 (probe).
- Row size: the 2024 M ≥ 4 CSV sample was 757 rows / 146,347 bytes ≈ 193 B/row (probe), so the whole 27k-row CSV is ≈ 5.3 MB; GeoJSON roughly 2–3× that (estimate).
- Magnitude types in the box: 2024 M ≥ 4 sample = 701 `mb`, 52 `mww`, 3 `mwr` (probe); the 1973–1990 large events are `ms`/`mw` (e.g. 1976 Moro Gulf usp0000hvb M7.9 `ms`; 1990 Luzon usp0004bxs M7.7 `mw`); 2012+ large events `mww` (2012 Samar M7.6, 2013 Bohol M7.1, 2023 Hinatuan M7.6, 2025 Cebu M6.9, 2025 Davao Oriental M7.4) (probe CSV, M ≥ 6.5, orderby=time-asc). USGS magnitude-type ranges (Mww ≥ ~5.0, Mwr ~4.0–6.5, Ms20 ~5.0–8.5, mb ~4.0–6.5, Ml ~2.0–6.5): https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/magnitude-types (accessed 2026-10-21). ComCat itself does not state a completeness threshold on https://earthquake.usgs.gov/data/comcat/ (accessed 2026-10-21); it refers to per-catalogue date ranges on its "Catalogs"/"Availability" pages. For a teleseismically located region like the Philippines the practical floor of ComCat is mb ≈ 4–4.5 since 1973 (PDE era); pre-1964 only M ≳ 5.5–6 via ISC-GEM/Centennial. **[U]** for the exact thresholds (standard seismological knowledge, not quoted from a page today).
- Real-time feeds: GeoJSON summary feeds, four windows (hour/day/week/month) × five thresholds (significant/4.5/2.5/1.0/all), updated every minute: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php (accessed 2026-10-21).
- Per-event product bundle (probe, `query?eventid=us6000rfwz&format=geojson`): origin, phase-data, moment-tensor (mww, mwc, mwb), finite-fault, shakemap, dyfi, losspager (PAGER alert "green" for the 2025 Davao Oriental M7.4), ground-failure, impact-text.

### 1.2 ISC Bulletin and ISC-GEM

- ISC Bulletin: "the definitive record of the Earth's seismicity", 1900–present; the Reviewed Bulletin runs ~24 months behind (currently to 2024-10-01); monthly files in ISF or PDF on download.isc.ac.uk / ftp.isc.ac.uk; FDSN event web service available (https://www.isc.ac.uk/iscbulletin/, accessed 2026-10-21). Citation is mandatory: "Data retrieved from the ISC Bulletin should always be cited. This includes use by academic or commercial organisations, as well as by individuals"; product citation "International Seismological Centre (2026), On-line Bulletin, https://doi.org/10.31905/D808B830" (https://www.isc.ac.uk/iscbulletin/citing.php, accessed 2026-10-21). No explicit licence text on the Bulletin pages; the Data Management Policy (https://www.isc.ac.uk/include/ISC-DMP_approved-by-GC2023.pdf, accessed 2026-10-21; text extracted locally) speaks of monthly ISF files stored "in an openly accessible place on the ISC ftp-site" and of a free ISC Dataset Repository, but contains no licence name. Treat redistribution of Bulletin extracts as "allowed in practice, cite always, no stated licence" **[U]**.
- ISC FDSN probe (http://www.isc.ac.uk/fdsnws/event/1/query?…, 2026-10-21): the PH box, 2015, M ≥ 3 returned 3,112 events; prime author MAN (= PHIVOLCS) for 1,920 of them, ISC 901, IDC 166, TAP 94, JMA 10. PHIVOLCS-authored magnitudes in that year: 1,716 `mb`, 203 `MS`, 1 `ML`. So the ISC Bulletin is the only bulk, machine-readable route to PHIVOLCS hypocentres for pre-2018 years (see 1.4), and it carries PHIVOLCS's own magnitude labels.
- ISC-GEM Global Instrumental Earthquake Catalogue (1904–2021): M ≥ 5.5 globally, continental events down to Mw 5.0 for 1964+ (https://www.isc.ac.uk/iscgem/, accessed 2026-10-21). Download page: "The Catalogue is protected by the Copyright (C) 2025 International Seismological Centre and GEM Foundation. You may use this work under the terms of the CC-BY-SA 3.0 (unported)"; CSV main + supplementary catalogue + KMZ; DOI http://doi.org/10.31905/D808B825 (https://www.isc.ac.uk/iscgem/download.php, accessed 2026-10-21). The download form requires name, email, affiliation, country and a CAPTCHA (https://www.isc.ac.uk/iscgem/request_catalogue.php, accessed 2026-10-21), i.e. a form but not an account. Note ISC-GEM is already inside ComCat for pre-1970 PH events (probe), so the share-alike question can be side-stepped by taking the public-domain ComCat copy.

### 1.3 Global CMT

- "more than 25,000 moment tensors for earthquakes since 1976", systematic for M > 5 with a 3–4 month delay, quick CMTs for M > 5.5; ASCII files; no licence text, "we greatly appreciate users of the catalog including appropriate citations" (https://www.globalcmt.org/, accessed 2026-10-21). Citation page asks for Dziewonski et al. 1981 and Ekström et al. 2012 and has no redistribution statement (https://www.globalcmt.org/CMTcite.html, accessed 2026-10-21). PH subset size: not counted (form-based search) **[U]**, likely 1,500–3,000 tensors (estimate). USGS moment tensors (mww/mwc/mwb) are in ComCat anyway, public domain.

### 1.4 PHIVOLCS earthquake information pages

- https://earthquake.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/ (accessed 2026-10-21): heading "PHIVOLCS LATEST EARTHQUAKE INFORMATION", parameters "determined using data from the Philippine Seismic Network", PST = UTC+8. Columns: Date-Time (PST), Latitude (°N), Longitude (°E), Depth (km), Mag, Location (e.g. "096 km N 29° E of Mapanas (Northern Samar)"). It is an HTML table (1,297 `<tr>` in the current page, probe), each row linking to a per-event bulletin page such as `2026_Earthquake_Information/October/2026_1001_0006_B1.html`. Rows "with their date and time in blue have reported and recorded intensities". No download or API link, no terms-of-use or copyright notice on the page.
- Archive: the page links monthly archive pages `EQLatest-Monthly/<YYYY>/<YYYY>_<Month>.html` for 2018 through 2026 only (probe: years 2018–2026). Directory listing is forbidden (403) and there is no older archive on this host; pre-2018 PHIVOLCS hypocentres must come via ISC (1.2) or a data request.
- Monthly volumes (probe, rows = per-event bulletin links): Jan 2018 373; Jun 2019 777; Jun 2021 1,536; Jun 2023 1,039; Jun 2024 1,482; Jun 2025 1,635; Oct 2025 5,585 rows (M ≥ 4: 172; M ≥ 3: 936; min M1.0, max M7.4; 10.6 MB of HTML); Jun 2026 4,768 (9.1 MB). A rough total for 2018–Oct 2026 is 150–200k rows (my estimate). Monthly pages are 0.5–10.6 MB each.
- Per-event bulletin format (probe, 2018_0430_0420_B2.html and 2025_0930_1359_B3F.html): a Word-exported HTML page with "EARTHQUAKE INFORMATION NO. : n", Date/Time, Location (lat/lon + distance/bearing from a town), Depth of Focus (Km), Origin (TECTONIC), Magnitude (**"Ms 1.9"** in the 2018 example; **"Mw 6.9"** in the 2025 Cebu bulletin), Reported Intensities (PEIS, by municipality), Expecting Damage, Expecting Aftershocks, Issued On. The "Ms" label on a M1.9 event shows the label is a house convention, not a 20-s surface-wave magnitude.
- Latest-earthquake feeds: the WordPress site has an RSS feed for the earthquake-information category (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/category/earthquake-information/feed/, HTTP 200, `application/rss+xml`, probe) which carries posts, not a structured event feed; PHIVOLCS also posts bulletins on X/Facebook **[U]**. There is no documented JSON/FDSN endpoint at PHIVOLCS; community scrapers of the HTML exist **[U]** (not searched).
- Bulk catalogue: the PHIVOLCS Citizen's Charter 2025 lists "Catalogue: Earthquake Data" as a SOEPD external service (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/PHIVOLCS_Citizens_Charter_2025_2nd_Edition_112825.pdf, from search snippet; the PDF URL returned 404 on direct HEAD on 2026-10-21, so **[U]**). An FOI request for a Region IV-A catalogue was marked denied (https://foi.gov.ph/requests/earthquake-catalogue-and-seismicity-map-in-region-iv-a, search snippet, accessed 2026-10-21).
- Special reports page lists only 2012–2019 events, each behind a Google Form (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/special-reports/, accessed 2026-10-21); nothing yet for the 2025 events.

### 1.5 The 2025 events (verified)

- **30 Sep 2025 Cebu (Bogo) earthquake.** USGS us6000rdrz: 2025-09-30T13:59:43Z, 11.1557 N 124.111 E, depth 10 km, **M 6.9 mww**, "8 km ESE of Calape, Philippines" (probe, FDSN CSV). PHIVOLCS Earthquake Information No. 3: 30 Sep 2025 09:59:44 PM PST, 11.10 N 124.14 E, "019 km N 70° E of City Of Bogo (Cebu)", depth 005 km, **Mw 6.9**, Intensity VII at Daanbantayan, Bogo, Medellin, San Remigio, Tabuelan and Cebu City (https://earthquake.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/2025_Earthquake_Information/September/2025_0930_1359_B3F.html, accessed 2026-10-21). PHIVOLCS first reported 6.7 then revised to 6.9; source: the newly named Bogo Bay Fault; NDRRMC 79 deaths and 599 injuries as of 24 Oct 2025; 160,662 houses damaged, 7,410 destroyed; ≥ 13,584 aftershocks by 21 Oct 2025, strongest M5.8 on 13 Oct; "deadliest earthquake in the Philippines since the 2013 Bohol earthquake" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cebu_earthquake, accessed 2026-10-21; death-toll figures differ by date: 76 per NDRRMC on 16 Oct per https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/reported-deaths-from-cebu-quake-now-76-ndrrmc, search snippet).
- **10 Oct 2025 Davao Oriental doublet.** USGS us6000rfwz: 2025-10-10T01:43:59Z, 7.2866 N 126.6902 E, depth 59.4 km, **M 7.4 mww**, "12 km E of Santiago"; second shock us6000rg2r 11:12:05Z, 7.2127 N 126.6578 E, 47 km, **M 6.7 mww**; and us6000rgkr 11 Oct M6.0 (probe). PHIVOLCS: 09:43 PST near Manay, first 7.6 then 7.5 then 7.4, depth 23 km (after a first 10 km); tsunami warning for Davao Oriental, Dinagat, Surigao del Norte/Sur, Leyte, Southern Leyte, Eastern Samar, cancelled 13:43 PST; ~30 cm wave at Tandag (PHIVOLCS cancellation bulletin https://tsunami.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/Tsunami_Information/2025_Tsunami_Information/Oct/10/20231010_0143z_Advisory_2_2.pdf and press reports, search snippets, accessed 2026-10-21). Philippine Trench, oblique reverse faulting, USGS finite fault ~90 × 55 km with up to 4.7 m slip; NDRRMC 10 deaths, 176 injuries as of 24 Oct 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davao_Oriental_earthquakes, accessed 2026-10-21). Note the depth disagreement (PHIVOLCS 23 km vs USGS 59 km) is itself a good honesty exhibit.

## 2. Faults and hazard

- **PHIVOLCS active faults.** The Philippine Fault Zone maps (Kyoto University + PHIVOLCS since 2003, aerial photographs ≥ 1:30,000 plotted on 1:50,000 NAMRIA sheets, ~90 % of the on-land PFZ mapped, "available on this website and upon request to PHIVOLCS-DOST", cite Tsutsumi & Perez 2013; no licence text) (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/philippine-fault-zone-maps/, accessed 2026-10-21). Hazard-map downloads are **KMZ only** via GISWeb (https://gisweb.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/gisweb/earthquake-volcano-related-hazard-gis-information) or HazardHunterPH "Download Maps" (PNG/PDF/KMZ); "These maps may be revised as new information becomes available"; no copyright/licence text (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/hazard-maps-2/, accessed 2026-10-21). **Shapefiles are not public**: multiple FOI requests for active-fault shapefiles are marked denied, with PHIVOLCS replying that a signed Data User Agreement or MOU is required and the request must go through its frontline-services form choosing "Vector/*.shp" (https://foi.gov.ph/requests/shapefile-of-active-faults-in-the-philippines, https://foi.gov.ph/requests/active-faults-latest-shapefile, https://foi.gov.ph/requests/active-faults-valley-fault-system, search snippets, accessed 2026-10-21).
- **Valley Fault System atlas.** Released 18 May 2015; large-scale maps for 19 cities/municipalities (17 on the West Valley Fault: Quezon City, Marikina, Pasig, Makati, Taguig, Muntinlupa, San Jose del Monte, Norzagaray, Doña Remedios Trinidad, San Pedro, Biñan, Santa Rosa, Cabuyao, Calamba, GMA, Carmona, Silang; 2 on the East Valley Fault: San Mateo, Rodriguez); WVF can generate M7.2; movement interval "roughly between 400-600 years", last movement most likely 1658 (https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/story/489334/scitech/science/list-barangays-in-metro-manila-nearby-provinces-near-earthquake-fault, accessed 2026-10-21). On the PHIVOLCS site "The Valleyfault System ATLAS" sits on the site-response atlases page with a Google Drive image link; the other atlases are behind Google Forms; no stated format or terms (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/specific-earthquake-project-site-response-atlases/, accessed 2026-10-21). Whether the atlas PDFs are freely redistributable: **[U]** (no licence anywhere).
- **PHIVOLCS FaultFinder** (https://faultfinder.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/) did not respond to a probe (connection failed, 2026-10-21) **[U]**; it is the public "how far am I from a fault" tool and was a DAP award winner together with the VFS atlas (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/dap-awards-center-of-excellence-on-public-sector-productivity-to-dost-phivolcs/, per site search, accessed 2026-10-21).
- **GEM Global Active Faults Database.** Licence "CC-BY-SA-4.0"; formats GeoJSON, GeoPackage, KML, Shapefile; compiled from 26 regional datasets; the Philippines entry is Peñarubia et al. (2019), the PH PSHA model, not a PHIVOLCS trace set; citation Styron & Pagani 2020, Earthquake Spectra, doi 10.1177/8755293020944182 (https://github.com/GEMScienceTools/gem-global-active-faults, accessed 2026-10-21). File sizes: `gem_active_faults.geojson` 12.3 MB, `gem_active_faults_harmonized.geojson` 10.6 MB (GitHub API probe); the PH clip will be well under 1 MB (estimate). **Share-alike implication:** any derived fault layer (re-projected, clipped, styled attributes) must be published under CC BY-SA 4.0; the app's own code and the unrelated catalogue layers are not affected if kept as separate works, but a merged "hazard GeoPackage" would be SA. The GEM product page returned 404 (https://www.globalquakemodel.org/product/global-active-faults, 2026-10-21).
- **PHIVOLCS Earthquake Intensity Scale (PEIS).** Ten levels: I Scarcely Perceptible, II Slightly Felt, III Weak, IV Moderately Strong, V Strong, VI Very Strong, VII Destructive, VIII Very Destructive, IX Devastating, X Completely Devastating (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/phivolcs-earthquake-intensity-scale-2/, accessed 2026-10-21). Adopted 1996 after the 1990 Luzon earthquake, replacing Rossi-Forel (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHIVOLCS_earthquake_intensity_scale, accessed 2026-10-21). PEIS ≠ MMI; USGS ShakeMaps are MMI, PHIVOLCS bulletins are PEIS: a mapping table is needed and must be labelled approximate.
- **GEM Global Seismic Hazard Map v2026.1** (replaces v2023.1): PGA with 10 % exceedance in 50 years on rock VS30 800 m/s; licences: base-layer vector file listed as CC BY-SA-NC 4.0 in one place and "CC BY-SA 4.0/CC BY-NC-SA 4.0" in another, Geoviewer CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, PDF poster and PNG CC BY-SA 4.0; "Any deviation from these terms incur in license infringement"; the full 42-layer set and commercial use require a licence request (https://www.globalquakemodel.org/product/global-seismic-hazard-map and https://www.globalquakemodel.org/gem-maps/global-earthquake-hazard-map, accessed 2026-10-21). So: the **data layer is non-commercial share-alike**; a free portfolio site is fine, but a re-hosted copy must carry CC BY-NC-SA and must not be mixed into a CC BY-SA/public-domain bundle. The GEM Philippines national model page returned 404 (https://www.globalquakemodel.org/product/philippines-hazard-model, 2026-10-21) **[U]**.
- **USGS ShakeMap.** "near-real-time maps of ground motion and shaking intensity following significant earthquakes"; ShakeMap Atlas ≈ 14,100 events 1900–2019 (https://earthquake.usgs.gov/data/shakemap/, accessed 2026-10-21). Public domain (USGS policy above). Per-event product contents for us6000rfwz (probe, bytes): `grid.xml` 9,614,123; `uncertainty.xml` 9,735,950; `raster.zip` 6,734,998; `shape.zip` 6,033,126; `shake_result.hdf` 18,802,728; `shakemap.kmz` 208,910; contour GeoJSON `cont_mmi.json` 16,417 (plus pga/pgv/psa 10–14 KB each); CoverageJSON `coverage_mmi_low_res.covjson` 40,918 / medium 159,312 / high 632,912; `stationlist.json` 599,694; `info.json` 7,255; `intensity_overlay.png` 56,946; `rupture.json` 678; `attenuation_curves.json` 60,643. The 1990 Luzon M7.7 (usp0004bxs) has an **Atlas ShakeMap** (maxmmi 9.28, 58 content files) (probe), so historical damaging quakes have shaking rasters too.
- **MMEIRS (2002–2004, JICA–PHIVOLCS–MMDA).** Published March 2004; worst-case West Valley Fault M7.2 scenario: ~170,000 residential houses collapsed and 340,000 damaged, "34,000 deaths and 114,000 injuries" from collapse plus "additional 18,000 deaths" from fires (JICA final report https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11763745_04.pdf and https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/264258/what-if-7-6-quake-hit-metro-manila, from search snippets; the JICA PDF fetched is a binary stream I could not parse, so the exact quotes are **[U]**). The 2015 press figures are 31,000–33,500 deaths (GMA article above); the 2026 OCD contingency plan says "death toll over 50,000" and ≥ 12 % of residential buildings heavily damaged (https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/991930/ocd-2026-plan-earthquake-metro-manila/story/, search snippet, accessed 2026-10-21). Three numbers for one scenario is a presentable honesty point. The "Model 08" label could not be confirmed **[U]**.
- **REDAS.** "a software developed by PHIVOLCS in 2002"; "Interested parties need to enter into a Memorandum of Agreement with PHIVOLCS"; "The software and training are free"; modules include SHAke, SWIFT, FLOAT, TsuSIM, CropDAT (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/redas-activities/, accessed 2026-10-21). Not usable without an MOA, and its outputs are not public data.
- **HazardHunterPH / GeoRiskPH.** HazardHunterPH assesses seismic, volcanic and hydrometeorological hazards for a point, "All hazard information used for assessment has been generated by government agencies", "© 2018 - 2020 DOST-PHIVOLCS, All rights reserved"; advanced features for partner organisations with GeoRiskPH SSO accounts; no public API; a "Terms of Use" link exists whose text was not captured (https://hazardhunter.georisk.gov.ph/, accessed 2026-10-21). https://georisk.gov.ph/ failed TLS verification on fetch (2026-10-21) **[U]**. Official hazard-assessment reports are paid (https://has.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/, per the hazard-maps page). Conclusion: HazardHunterPH is a product to link to, not a source to scrape; its "All rights reserved" is the clearest statement PHIVOLCS makes about any of its web data.

## 4. Tsunami

- **PHIVOLCS tsunami hazard maps**: KMZ via GISWeb/HazardHunterPH, by region/province/municipality, "availability of hazard map may vary depending on the location"; two generations exist, tsunami-prone-area maps (30 provinces, 2007–2013) and inundation maps (29 provinces, 2018–2022), with 8 coastal provinces still unmapped per a 2025 PHIVOLCS workshop deck (https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/tsunami-hazard-maps-3/, accessed 2026-10-21; province counts from https://oceanexpert.org/downloadFile/61058, search snippet). - **NOAA NCEI/WDS Global Historical Tsunami Database**: "range in date from 2000 B.C. to the present", > 2,200 source events with validity > 0, > 27,000 run-up locations; event validity −1 erroneous, 0 seiche, 1–4 increasing confidence; access through HazEL (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/global-historical-data, accessed 2026-10-21); cite "doi:10.7289/V5PN93H7" (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis, accessed 2026-10-21). Luzon, 1645 Manila M8 with 600 deaths), and `…/api/v1/earthquakes?country=PHILIPPINES` → **233 significant earthquakes** (probe, 2026-10-21). As a US-government work it is public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105; NCEI's own pages state no licence (the NOAA disclaimer page did not carry the public-domain sentence on fetch, so the explicit statement is **[U]**).

## 5. Scientific honesty

- **Completeness.** ComCat in the PH box: 1–31 events per year 1901–1944, essentially all ISC-GEM M ≥ 5.5 relocations; mb ≥ ~4 only from the PDE era (1973+) (probe; threshold **[U]**). PHIVOLCS's public list starts 2018 and goes down to M1.0 (probe, Oct 2025: 5,585 rows, 172 with M ≥ 4). The PHIVOLCS network grew from ~101 stations in 2019 (ABS-CBN explainer, search snippet https://news.abs-cbn.com/news/04/24/19/why-do-phivolcs-usgs-data-differ, page blocked on fetch, **[U]**) to the current "Philippine Seismic Network" (page 404 on fetch), so the lower-magnitude count rises with time for instrumental, not tectonic, reasons: a time-series of counts must be gated at a constant completeness magnitude (M ≥ 4.5 or 5 for the century, lower only for 2018+).
- **Magnitude inconsistency.** Same events carry `mb` (USGS 2024: 93 % of M ≥ 4 rows), `mww`, `ms`, `mw`, and PHIVOLCS's house "Ms"/"Mw" labels; PHIVOLCS-authored magnitudes in ISC 2015 are 89 % `mb`, 11 % `MS` (probe). The 2025 Davao Oriental M7.4 is 23 km deep for PHIVOLCS and 59 km for USGS; the Cebu event is 5 km vs 10 km (section 1.5). Rule: store the magnitude type with every row, never mix scales in one histogram, and show PHIVOLCS and USGS solutions side by side rather than merging.
- **Location uncertainty.** ComCat exposes `horizontalError`, `depthError`, `magError`, `nst`, `gap`, `dmin` per row (probe CSV header): e.g. us6000rfwz horizontalError 7.79 km, depthError 4.3 km. Pre-1964 ISC-GEM locations are to the degree (probe: 1901 Luzon at 14.0000, 122.0000). Draw error ellipses or at least fade old epicentres.
- **No forecasting.** USGS: "No. Neither the USGS nor any other scientists have ever predicted a major earthquake"; a prediction must state when, where and how large; probabilities "comparable to climate probabilities and weather forecasts" are different (https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/can-you-predict-earthquakes, accessed 2026-10-21). PHIVOLCS repeatedly states earthquakes "cannot be predicted" and debunks dated warnings (April 2025 "April 22" hoax, https://abs-cbn.com/news/nation/2025/4/16/phivolcs-earthquakes-can-t-be-predicted-april-22-warning-is-fake-1113; VERA Files fact-check of a viral "risk prediction map", Sept 2026, https://verafiles.org/articles/fact-check-earthquakes-cannot-be-predicted-viral-risk-prediction-map-fake; search snippets, accessed 2026-10-21). A viral fake "risk map" being fact-checked a month ago is a direct warning for this project: a pretty national map with hot colours can be screenshot and recirculated as a prediction.
- **Showing recurrence without prediction.** Show (a) Gutenberg–Richter rate per region with the completeness cut explicit; (b) the WVF paleoseismic facts as PHIVOLCS states them ("four times in 1,400 years, 400–600-year interval, last 1658") with no countdown; (c) GEM/PHIVOLCS probabilistic hazard as "PGA with 10 % chance of exceedance in 50 years", with the sentence that this is a long-run average, not a date. Avoid "overdue", "due", "imminent".
- **What a non-authority must not do during an event.** Do not publish magnitudes/epicentres before PHIVOLCS/USGS bulletins; do not issue or echo tsunami advice (PHIVOLCS is the sole tsunami-warning authority for the Philippines); do not run a "latest earthquakes" ticker that could be mistaken for official information; link to PHIVOLCS bulletins and NDRRMC; show the authority's revision history (6.7→6.9, 7.6→7.4) instead of your own numbers; put a date-stamped "this is a historical catalogue explorer, not a warning service" notice on every view.

## 6. Existing products and public interest

- USGS Latest Earthquakes map and per-event pages (ShakeMap, DYFI, PAGER): the default global reference; everything in ComCat (section 1.1).
- PHIVOLCS site: HTML bulletin lists (section 1.4), HazardHunterPH, FaultFinder, GISWeb KMZ downloads (section 2).
- EarthScope Interactive Earthquake Browser (formerly IRIS IEB): USGS-sourced, 10–25,000 events on a 3-D rotating map, time range from ~1970, animation, plate boundaries (https://www.earthscope.org/app/ieb/, accessed 2026-10-21).
- Temblor: iOS/Android/web "Check My Risk" for earthquake, flood, wildfire, liquefaction, landslide, tsunami; commercial models, API and building-level score for insurers (https://temblor.net/, accessed 2026-10-21).
- Earthquake Track: text lists and counts ("474 in the past 365 days" M ≥ 1.5 for the Philippines), "Data: USGS", no maps or time series on the page (https://earthquaketrack.com/p/philippines/recent, accessed 2026-10-21).
- Public interest: "The Big One" is a decade-plus running topic; 2026 alone has the OCD Harmonized National Contingency Plan for Greater Metro Manila, the 18 June 2026 metro-wide drill, MMDA's push for quarterly drills, and the 30 Sep–2 Oct 2026 Exercise Sanlakas 02-2026 on a WVF scenario (https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/18/big-one-readiness-tested-in-metro-manila-earthquake-drills, https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/30/pnp-joins-afp-pcg-in-big-one-earthquake-drill, search snippets, accessed 2026-10-21). The 2025 Cebu (79 dead) and Davao Oriental (tsunami warning, doublet) events are the most recent national-news earthquakes (section 1.5). Nobody currently offers a Philippines-specific, century-long, PHIVOLCS-plus-USGS explorer with the magnitude-type and completeness caveats made visible; that gap is the pitch.

## 8. Volumes and free-tier feasibility

- Catalogue: ComCat PH box 27,334 rows ≈ 5.3 MB CSV (probe); PHIVOLCS 2018–2026 ≈ 150–200k rows (estimate) ≈ 20–30 MB CSV, parsed from ~0.5–10 MB monthly HTML pages (probe); ISC Bulletin PH since 1964 at all magnitudes: likely 100k–300k rows **[U]** (2015 M ≥ 3 alone was 3,112, probe). All of this fits a few Parquet/GeoJSON files far under Cloudflare Pages' 25 MiB per-asset and 20,000-file limits (https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/, accessed 2026-10-21).
- ShakeMaps: per event, the light products (contours 10–16 KB each, CoverageJSON low-res 40–60 KB, overlay PNG 57 KB, info.json 7 KB) total ≈ 0.3 MB; the heavy products (grid.xml 9.6 MB, raster.zip 6.7 MB, hdf 18.8 MB) ≈ 45 MB (probe). For the ~232 M ≥ 6.5 events (probe) light products ≈ 70 MB; full grids ≈ 10 GB. Light products on Pages, full grids only in the archive releases.
- Faults: GEM GAF 12.3 MB global, PH clip < 1 MB (estimate). GEM hazard vector base layer: global, probably tens of MB **[U]**; a PH clip is small.
- Exposure: WorldPop PH 100 m ≈ 15–30 MB **[U]**; Google Open Buildings PH, tens of millions of polygons, several GB **[U]** (Microsoft PH: 17.4 M polygons, probably 2–4 GB GeoJSONL **[U]**). These are the only genuinely large inputs and they should be pre-aggregated (per barangay or per 1 km cell) offline, not stored in Neon.
- Neon Free: 1 GB per project, 100 CU-hours, 5 GB egress, PostGIS available, scale-to-zero after 5 min (https://neon.com/pricing, accessed 2026-10-21). The catalogue plus aggregated exposure fits easily; raw building footprints do not. A static-first design (Parquet/PMTiles on Pages, DuckDB-WASM or a Worker) may not need Neon at all. Workers Free: 100,000 requests/day, 10 ms CPU (https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/, accessed 2026-10-21).
- Google Earth Engine: not required. Open Buildings and GHSL are also EE assets, but the exposure aggregation is doable with DuckDB/GeoPandas locally. Absence of a satellite/EE stage is a point against "big data" credentials compared with the night-lights project.

## 9. Technical showcase

- What makes it more than a point map: (1) **ShakeMap stacking**: sum the per-event MMI/PGA CoverageJSON or grids for all M ≥ 6 events since 1900 (Atlas + real-time, ≈ 600 events, probe) into "maximum observed shaking per cell" and "number of times ≥ MMI VI" layers; this is an empirical, honest counterpart to the GEM probabilistic map and does not exist for the Philippines publicly. (2) **Exposure computation**: intersect those layers (and the GEM PGA layer) with WorldPop/GHSL/building footprints to give people and buildings per shaking class per province, with the uncertainty from completeness carried through. (3) **Dual-catalogue reconciliation**: match PHIVOLCS and USGS/ISC solutions per event, show depth/magnitude/location offsets as distributions (the 23 km vs 59 km case), a thing no viewer does. (4) Tile pipeline: PMTiles for 200k points + fault lines + rasters, served statically.
- It is still point data with modest volumes; the "big" part is the raster stacking and footprint aggregation, both batch jobs, not a streaming or satellite pipeline.
- Overlap with the volcano candidate: same authority (PHIVOLCS), same bulletin-scraping problem (volcano bulletins are also WordPress/HTML pages, https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/volcano-bulletin2/, fetch returned 404 on 2026-10-21 **[U]**), same hazard-map KMZ channel, same HazardHunterPH terms. Building the PHIVOLCS archiving layer once would serve both; choosing both would look repetitive on a portfolio.

## 10. Risks

1. **"It reads like the USGS map."** The default mental model of an earthquake explorer is earthquake.usgs.gov; unless the product leads with the stacked-shaking/exposure and the reconciliation views, it will be judged as a re-skin.
2. **Thin satellite/remote-sensing angle.** No EO data, no EE; the portfolio already has a night-lights project for that, so this candidate adds breadth (hazard/geoscience) rather than depth in big-data tooling.
3. **Real-time trap.** Anything that updates during an event invites being used as a warning source; PHIVOLCS is the sole authority and actively fights fake predictions (section 5). A batch-updated historical explorer with a visible "last updated" date avoids this; a live ticker does not.
4. **PHIVOLCS data terms.** No open licence anywhere, shapefiles denied via FOI, "All rights reserved" on HazardHunterPH, and the public bulletin list only goes back to 2018: the archive of PHIVOLCS bulletins is a scrape with unclear redistribution rights.
5. **Licence mixing.** CC BY-SA (GEM faults, ISC-GEM), CC BY-NC-SA (GEM hazard), ODbL (Microsoft footprints) alongside public-domain USGS data; manageable only with per-source files.
